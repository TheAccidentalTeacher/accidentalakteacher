Wren and Sitka's Field Notebook

25 Nature Investigations for Curious Kids

Twenty five investigations. One pencil. Whatever is outside your door.

Each lesson teaches one real idea, gives your child something to do with it, and ends with a reason to step outside. No kit, no login, and no science background needed.

The teaching idea

Science that leaves the worksheet

This is a field notebook before it is a bundle. The page gives the idea enough structure to teach it. Then Wren and Sitka hand the question back to the real world.
  1. 01

    Read the teaching page

    Start together. The LEARN side carries the science your child needs for that investigation, in plain language and with the picture doing real work.

  2. 02

    Let your child try it

    The second page turns the idea into a drawing, comparison, model, or short explanation. The answer is never a hunt through another book.

  3. 03

    Take the prompt outside

    Every investigation ends with one small thing to notice outdoors. A cloud, a track, a leaf, a shadow, or the ground under your feet is enough.

  4. 04

    Use the grown-up help

    A short grown-up strip sits on every activity page, and the bundle includes the complete answer key. You do not need to remember fourth-grade science first.

Use one investigation in twenty minutes, stretch it across an afternoon, or follow a question into the next week. The notebook does not care where Tuesday went.

The full field list

25 things worth going outside to notice

Life science, earth science, and naturalist skills live in one notebook. The familiar school topics are here too: living and nonliving things, animal adaptations, butterfly life cycle, food chains and food webs, photosynthesis, types of rocks, soil, water cycle, clouds, weather and climate, moon phases, compass directions, and field journals.
  1. 01
    Life Science

    What Makes Something Alive?

    Field note: A thing has to do every job on the list, not just one or two. That is why fire and rivers fool people.

  2. 02
    Life Science

    Reading Tracks

    Field note: Claw marks are the fastest way to tell a dog family track from a cat family track. Cats retract, dogs do not.

  3. 03
    Life Science

    Beaks and What They Eat

    Field note: A beak is an adaptation. It matches the food that is actually there, because a bird whose beak suits its food feeds well and raises more chicks.

  4. 04
    Life Science

    Feathers and Flight

    Field note: Run your fingers the wrong way down a feather and it splits. Smooth it back and it seals. Those are the barbules hooking and unhooking.

  5. 05
    Life Science

    How Smart Is a Raven?

    Field note: Moving hidden food only makes sense if the raven understands that another animal saw it. That is a big idea called knowing what somebody else knows.

  6. 06
    Life Science

    The Complete Change

    Field note: The caterpillar and the butterfly eat different food on purpose. They are not competing with each other for the same meal.

  7. 07
    Life Science

    The Salmon's Journey

    Field note: The salmon carries ocean nutrients hundreds of miles inland and leaves them in a forest. Trees near salmon streams grow measurably faster.

  8. 08
    Life Science

    Who Eats Whom

    Field note: The arrow points toward the eater, because the arrow is showing where the energy went, not who is eating.

  9. 09
    Life Science

    It Is Not a Line

    Field note: The more arrows an animal has pointing away from it, the safer the web is if that animal disappears. Variety is a kind of insurance.

  10. 10
    Life Science

    Predator and Prey

    Field note: Eyes in front, born to hunt. Eyes on the side, born to hide. It works on almost every animal you will meet.

  11. 11
    Life Science

    Hiding in Plain Sight

    Field note: Camouflage says do not see me. Mimicry says you have seen me and you should be worried. They are opposite strategies.

  12. 12
    Life Science

    Three Ways to Survive Winter

    Field note: None of the three is best. Each one trades a different cost. Migration costs energy, hibernation costs time, and staying costs constant work.

  13. 13
    Life Science

    How a Tree Drinks

    Field note: The tree does not push water up. The leaves let water go, and the rest of the column follows because water sticks to itself.

  14. 14
    Life Science

    Reading Time in a Stump

    Field note: One year is one light band plus one dark band, not one line. Counting only the dark lines gets you the right answer, and counting every band gets you double.

  15. 15
    Life Science

    How Seeds Travel

    Field note: A berry is a bribe. The plant spends energy making sweet fruit purely to get an animal to carry the seed somewhere new.

  16. 16
    Life Science

    How a Leaf Eats Light

    Field note: Leaves are not green because green is useful. They are green because green is the color the plant throws away.

  17. 17
    Earth Science

    What Dirt Is Made Of

    Field note: Topsoil is not a renewable thing on a human timescale. Hundreds of years to make an inch, and a bad storm on bare ground can take it in an afternoon.

  18. 18
    Earth Science

    Three Kinds of Rock

    Field note: Look for layers and you are probably holding sedimentary. Look for visible crystals and you are probably holding igneous. Look for bands or a shiny squashed grain and you are probably holding metamorphic.

  19. 19
    Earth Science

    Water Goes in Circles

    Field note: The amount of water on Earth has not changed in a very long time. It is the same water, going around and around.

  20. 20
    Earth Science

    Reading the Sky

    Field note: Nimbus means rain. Cumulus means heaped up. Stratus means layered. Cirrus means a curl of hair. The names describe exactly what you are looking at.

  21. 21
    Earth Science

    Weather Is Not Climate

    Field note: One day is weather. Thirty years is climate. Anybody arguing about climate using yesterday is using the wrong size of evidence.

  22. 22
    Earth Science

    Why the Moon Changes Shape

    Field note: Phases are about your viewing angle, not about a shadow. Earth's shadow only lands on the moon during an eclipse, which happens rarely.

  23. 23
    Earth Science

    Finding North Without a Compass

    Field note: The North Star is not the brightest star in the sky. It is the one that does not move, and that is worth far more.

  24. 24
    Life Science

    Putting It All Together

    Field note: A habitat is an address. A niche is a job. Two animals can share an address, but not a job.

  25. 25
    Naturalist Skills

    Keeping a Field Journal

    Field note: Observation is what you saw. Inference is what you think it means. Keep them in separate sentences and you are doing science.
