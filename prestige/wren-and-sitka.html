WREN & SITKA'S
FIELD NOTEBOOK.

Twenty five investigations. One pencil. Whatever is outside your door.

25 Nature Investigations for Curious Kids

Each lesson teaches one real idea, gives your child something to do with it, and ends with a reason to step outside. No kit, no login, and no science background needed.

Any order. Any pace. Stop after the page or take the question outdoors.

01How the notebook works
01

Read the teaching page

Start together. The LEARN side carries the science your child needs for that investigation, in plain language and with the picture doing real work.

02

Let your child try it

The second page turns the idea into a drawing, comparison, model, or short explanation. The answer is never a hunt through another book.

03

Take the prompt outside

Every investigation ends with one small thing to notice outdoors. A cloud, a track, a leaf, a shadow, or the ground under your feet is enough.

04

Use the grown-up help

A short grown-up strip sits on every activity page, and the bundle includes the complete answer key. You do not need to remember fourth-grade science first.

Use one investigation in twenty minutes, stretch it across an afternoon, or follow a question into the next week. The notebook does not care where Tuesday went.

02The investigation list

25 REASONS
TO NOTICE.

Life science, earth science, and naturalist skills live in one notebook. The familiar school topics are here too: living and nonliving things, animal adaptations, butterfly life cycle, food chains and food webs, photosynthesis, types of rocks, soil, water cycle, clouds, weather and climate, moon phases, compass directions, and field journals.

01

life science

What Makes Something Alive?

A thing has to do every job on the list, not just one or two. That is why fire and rivers fool people.

02

life science

Reading Tracks

Claw marks are the fastest way to tell a dog family track from a cat family track. Cats retract, dogs do not.

03

life science

Beaks and What They Eat

A beak is an adaptation. It matches the food that is actually there, because a bird whose beak suits its food feeds well and raises more chicks.

04

life science

Feathers and Flight

Run your fingers the wrong way down a feather and it splits. Smooth it back and it seals. Those are the barbules hooking and unhooking.

05

life science

How Smart Is a Raven?

Moving hidden food only makes sense if the raven understands that another animal saw it. That is a big idea called knowing what somebody else knows.

06

life science

The Complete Change

The caterpillar and the butterfly eat different food on purpose. They are not competing with each other for the same meal.

07

life science

The Salmon's Journey

The salmon carries ocean nutrients hundreds of miles inland and leaves them in a forest. Trees near salmon streams grow measurably faster.

08

life science

Who Eats Whom

The arrow points toward the eater, because the arrow is showing where the energy went, not who is eating.

09

life science

It Is Not a Line

The more arrows an animal has pointing away from it, the safer the web is if that animal disappears. Variety is a kind of insurance.

10

life science

Predator and Prey

Eyes in front, born to hunt. Eyes on the side, born to hide. It works on almost every animal you will meet.

11

life science

Hiding in Plain Sight

Camouflage says do not see me. Mimicry says you have seen me and you should be worried. They are opposite strategies.

12

life science

Three Ways to Survive Winter

None of the three is best. Each one trades a different cost. Migration costs energy, hibernation costs time, and staying costs constant work.

13

life science

How a Tree Drinks

The tree does not push water up. The leaves let water go, and the rest of the column follows because water sticks to itself.

14

life science

Reading Time in a Stump

One year is one light band plus one dark band, not one line. Counting only the dark lines gets you the right answer, and counting every band gets you double.

15

life science

How Seeds Travel

A berry is a bribe. The plant spends energy making sweet fruit purely to get an animal to carry the seed somewhere new.

16

life science

How a Leaf Eats Light

Leaves are not green because green is useful. They are green because green is the color the plant throws away.

17

earth science

What Dirt Is Made Of

Topsoil is not a renewable thing on a human timescale. Hundreds of years to make an inch, and a bad storm on bare ground can take it in an afternoon.

18

earth science

Three Kinds of Rock

Look for layers and you are probably holding sedimentary. Look for visible crystals and you are probably holding igneous. Look for bands or a shiny squashed grain and you are probably holding metamorphic.

19

earth science

Water Goes in Circles

The amount of water on Earth has not changed in a very long time. It is the same water, going around and around.

20

earth science

Reading the Sky

Nimbus means rain. Cumulus means heaped up. Stratus means layered. Cirrus means a curl of hair. The names describe exactly what you are looking at.

21

earth science

Weather Is Not Climate

One day is weather. Thirty years is climate. Anybody arguing about climate using yesterday is using the wrong size of evidence.

22

earth science

Why the Moon Changes Shape

Phases are about your viewing angle, not about a shadow. Earth's shadow only lands on the moon during an eclipse, which happens rarely.

23

earth science

Finding North Without a Compass

The North Star is not the brightest star in the sky. It is the one that does not move, and that is worth far more.

24

life science

Putting It All Together

A habitat is an address. A niche is a job. Two animals can share an address, but not a job.

25

naturalist skills

Keeping a Field Journal

Observation is what you saw. Inference is what you think it means. Keep them in separate sentences and you are doing science.
