04Curriculum differentiation and assessment design

The Bill of Rights in Ten Pages

Ten amendments developed across four grade-band designs, with the demands of the task changing from early drawing and recognition to evidence-based argument.

Project type

Differentiated learning materials

Status

Four designed bands; three direct TPT listings

The deliverables

  • Ten amendments in a shared sequence
  • Four grade-band designs
  • Different reading and response demands
  • A public resource page and reading recommendations

Inspect the work

Explore the four grade bands

01 / The need

Keep the central idea. Change the work the learner does.

A family or co-op can study the same historical idea at several ages. The task is to preserve the meaning while changing the reading, reasoning, response, and support required from the learner.

02 / Design decisions

  1. Begin each amendment with a concrete complaint or event so the right is connected to the problem it addresses.
  2. Differentiate the response: early learners circle and draw; later bands work with explanation, tables, reasoning, and primary-source claims and counterclaims.
  3. Use a shared amendment sequence across the kindergarten to grade 2, grades 3 to 5, middle-school, and high-school designs.

03 / What you can inspect

The public page shows the ten-amendment sequence, the four grade-band approaches, and the progression in reading and reasoning demands. It also provides the current TPT edition links and a supporting reading list.
